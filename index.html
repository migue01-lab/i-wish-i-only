B1 English Lesson

Expressing Wishes & Regrets

Talking about things we want to change in our lives

1

Meet Sofia

Thinking about changes and things we want to be different

Sofia recently moved from a small, quiet town to a busy, noisy capital city for a new job. Every morning, sitting in heavy traffic with loud car horns outside her window, she thinks about her old life.

"I wish I lived in a quiet town again, and I wish I hadn't rushed into this apartment so quickly."

Have you ever felt like you wanted to change something in your life right now? Or maybe wished you had done something differently in the past?

Discussion Questions for Students

1. What is one thing about your daily routine or city that you would like to change?

2. If a student says "I wish to have more money," how can we help them rephrase it naturally using "I wish I had..."?

3. Why do we talk about imaginary or past situations when expressing our feelings?

2

Grammar Structure

How to form present wishes and past regrets

For Present Wishes (Wanting to change now): Use I wish / If only + Simple Past.
Example: "I wish I lived in a quiet town." (She lives in a noisy city now.)

For Past Regrets (Wanting to change the past): Use I wish / If only + Past Perfect (had + past participle).
Example: "I wish I had studied harder." (She didn't study hard before.)

1. Sofia wants to change her living situation right now. Which sentence is correct?

2. Sofia regrets missing her train yesterday. Which sentence is correct?

Personal Practice

Write your own sentences about changes and past choices

4

Pair Speaking Activity

Share your wishes and talk with a partner

Work with a partner. Read your sentences aloud to each other and discuss the reasons behind your choices.

Step 1: Share your present wishes with your partner.

Step 2: Tell your partner about a past regret using "I wish I had...".

Step 3: Ask your partner follow-up questions to keep the conversation going.
